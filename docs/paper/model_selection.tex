\subsection{Model Selection}
\label{model_selection}

Section~\ref{model_choice} set four requirements: German synthesis, cloning from
minimal data, low latency, and the option of self-hosting for privacy. The first
three are met by all three candidates. Each renders German, each clones from a
reference of a few seconds, and each returns audio faster than it is spoken
(service RTF $0.14$ to $0.39$). The requirements narrow the field but do not
decide between the candidates, which is what the evaluation is for.

Two criteria separate them (Table~\ref{tab:model_selection}). Perception rules
out Qwen3: despite the highest speaker similarity it is rated clearly less
natural than every other condition and is detected most reliably in the Turing
test. Licence and reliability rule out Voxtral, which matches S2 Pro
perceptually but is non-commercial and left three of sixteen texts unusable; a
system that fails on one text in five cannot replace a voice. S2 Pro is rated as
natural as the genuine recordings, rendered every text, and runs at a service
RTF of $0.23$. It is recommended not because it sounds better than Voxtral,
which the data cannot show, but because it is the only candidate that meets
every requirement at once.

The fourth requirement is not settled here. All three systems were evaluated
through their providers' APIs, so the enrolment recording left the local machine
in every case. Whether S2 Pro can be operated on private infrastructure has to
be established before deployment.
